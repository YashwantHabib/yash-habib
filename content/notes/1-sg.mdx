# From Intern to Software Engineer

Six months ago, I walked into Scientific Games as an intern with more questions than answers. Today, I get to call myself a Software Engineer.

Looking back, it's funny how quickly things changed.

## The Beginning

Joining Scientific Games felt surreal.

I've always loved the intersection of games and technology, so getting the opportunity to work in an industry I'd admired for years felt like a dream come true. At the time, everything was new—new people, new tools, new workflows, and countless things I had yet to understand.

There was a lot to learn, but that's exactly what made it exciting.

## Learning Every Day

The first few weeks were less about writing code and more about learning how everything fit together.

I quickly realized that building software—especially software used by thousands of people—isn't just about solving problems. It's about communication, collaboration, and understanding the decisions behind every line of code.

Every challenge taught me something new, whether it was a technical concept, a better way to approach a problem, or simply how experienced engineers think.

## Where Code Meets Creativity

One of my favorite things about working at Scientific Games is how naturally it brings together two things I've always enjoyed: engineering and creativity.

Growing up, I spent countless hours playing games like Mario and Contra, completely fascinated by the worlds someone had imagined and built. Years later, getting the chance to contribute—even in a small way—to an industry that inspired me as a kid still feels incredibly rewarding.

## The Plot Twist

Somewhere along the way, the unfamiliar slowly became familiar.

Training turned into real work.

Small tasks turned into bigger responsibilities.

Then came the email I'd quietly been hoping for.

I was offered a full-time position as a **Software Engineer**.

Safe to say... I smiled for the rest of the day.

## Looking Back

If there's one thing this journey has taught me, it's that curiosity compounds.

Showing up every day, asking questions, making mistakes, and continuing to learn eventually adds up in ways you don't always notice at the time.

There's still so much I don't know, and that's probably my favorite part.

This isn't the finish line.

It's simply the beginning of the next chapter.

---

I'm grateful to everyone at Scientific Games who made this journey possible, and I'm excited for everything that's still ahead.
