# My Story

I think my love for building things started long before I wrote my first line of code.

When I was a kid, my parents bought me one of those little handheld consoles with cartridges that proudly claimed to have *1000 games in one*.

  src="/images/me/console.jpg"

  caption="The tiny console that unknowingly started everything."

Every evening after school, I'd spend hours playing games like **Super Mario** and **Contra**. At the time, I had no idea what game engines were or how software worked—I just knew I was fascinated by these tiny worlds someone had built.

Looking back, I think that's where it all started. I wasn't just playing games. I was constantly wondering,

> *"How did someone make this?"*

That question has never really left me.



Years later, I discovered programming.

It started with simple console applications, then websites, then mobile applications, and eventually projects much bigger than I ever imagined building. The feeling, though, hasn't changed since those evenings with that tiny console. I still enjoy building things that make people smile, solve interesting problems, or simply teach me something new.



Today I'm a software engineer at [Scientific Games](/notes/scientific-games), where I get to solve interesting problems every day while learning from incredibly talented people. Most of what you see on this website exists because I wanted to understand something a little better than I did yesterday.


## Beyond Programming
I'm probably playing story-driven games, a few competitive ones (mostly Valorant), sketching, or thinking about the next project I'd like to build.


Thanks for reading.
